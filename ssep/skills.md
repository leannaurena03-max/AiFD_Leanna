# SSEP Mission Patch Generator

## Purpose

Build a mission patch generator based on Leanna Urena’s original sketch.
The patch answers: “What message would Baruch send into space?”

Core message:
Collaboration and repeated experimentation lead to new discoveries
along the road of curiosity.

The attached poster generator is context for controls, generative
variation, and exporting. Develop the patch tool around this sketch.

## Visual description

A road begins wide at the bottom and narrows toward an eye near the
center. Its perspective creates a sense of movement toward discovery.
A broken centerline makes the road recognizable.

Three circular forms surround the eye: one above it and two below,
on opposite sides. Each contains a puzzle piece. The pieces represent
individual contributions to a shared research effort. The circles
suggest floating forms in space.

Curved arrows connect the circular forms into a clockwise cycle.
They represent experimentation: testing, learning, revising, and
trying again.

The eye represents curiosity, observation, and discovery. Draw it as
a simplified symbol with an almond-shaped outline, iris, and pupil.
Avoid realistic eyelashes or small details that disappear when reduced.

The road, eye, puzzle pieces, and arrows are essential. Their
relationships must remain recognizable in every generated version.

## Format and hierarchy

- Every patch is square: 3.5 × 3.5 inches.
- Use a 1000 × 1000 logical drawing area.
- Export PNG files at 1050 × 1050 pixels or larger.
- Keep all artwork inside a clear border and safe margin.
- Give the central road and eye the strongest visual emphasis.
- Make the three puzzle-piece circles secondary.
- Keep arrows visible without overwhelming the other symbols.
- Use strong silhouettes and consistent line weights.
- Design for readability at the final physical size.

## Text

The sketch contains no lettering. Proposed editable defaults:
- Top: BARUCH
- Bottom: SSEP

Use bold Arial or Helvetica. Keep text horizontal and away from
the symbols. Allow blank labels. Fit text within the safe area.
Do not invent a mission number, experiment title, or official logo.

## Color

The sketch does not specify color. Use these proposed starting palettes:
- Space: navy, cream, green.
- Earth: deep green, cream, muted gold.
- Monochrome: cream and near-black.

Keep palettes limited and maintain clear contrast.
Color changes must preserve symbol readability.

## Generative rules

Preserve:
- A road leading upward toward the eye.
- One central eye.
- Three circles containing puzzle pieces.
- A clockwise cycle of arrows.
- A square output and protected text areas.

Vary within controlled limits:
- Horizontal spread and vertical spacing of the circles.
- Puzzle-piece rotations.
- Circle size.
- Road width.
- Eye size.
- Line weight.
- Approved color palettes.

Generate meaningful geometric variations, not only color changes.
Prevent symbols from colliding with labels or leaving the border.
The road may pass in front of part of the lower orbit.

## Interface

Use a white background, near-black text, and green controls.
Use small bold Helvetica headings and Arial body text.
Provide:
- A square patch preview.
- Editable top and bottom labels.
- Palette and composition choices.
- Controls for road width, eye size, and line weight.
- A Generate variation button.
- A Reset button.
- A Download PNG button.

Keep the interface responsive and usable with a keyboard.

## Rendering and export

Use self-contained browser drawing with no required external assets.
Export the same artwork displayed in the preview.
Exclude interface controls from the exported image.
Include 300 dpi PNG resolution metadata for a nominal
3.5-inch square at 1050 × 1050 pixels.

## Review

Check that:
- All four essential symbols remain recognizable.
- Variations change the composition while preserving its meaning.
- Text stays inside its reserved area.
- PNG dimensions and resolution metadata are correct.
- The design remains legible when reduced to 3.5 inches.
