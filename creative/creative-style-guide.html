02 / The idea

A reason
to linger.

The best part of the holiday is the time you spend together.

Product & audience

Keep the winter layering edit visible: fleece, knitwear, and overshirts with navy, cream, and earthy colors. Show how pieces layer and move in everyday life.

Speak to adults who enjoy comfortable, expressive clothing and time with friends. Include a range of ages, skin tones, body types, and personal styles.

Place & feeling

Use the East River waterfront as the recurring setting: bridge steelwork, river railings, weathered benches, and the Manhattan skyline.

Blend cool outdoor blues with warm sunset light and small holiday details. The feeling is relaxed, welcoming, and lived in.

Translate the moodboard into a consistent system. Its torn paper and overlapping photographs inspire the process presentation; finished ads should usually give one photograph room to breathe.

05 / Identity direction

A quiet
signature.

Wordmark & lockup

The next logo asset should explore friendly, fluid lettering inspired by the moodboard's signature feeling. Pair the approved campaign wordmark with “Stay a Little Longer” in Georgia; do not redraw or change the lettering between assets.

The serif nameplate introduces this guide. The final campaign wordmark will be developed as a separate, consistent identity asset.

Placement & protection

Use one approved lockup per ad, in navy, evergreen, or cream. Keep clear space equal to the capital F height on all sides. Place it on a calm area or a solid panel.

Do not stretch, rotate, outline, add shadows, or place it across a face. Until a final asset exists, reserve a clean logo area in generated compositions.

06 / Photography

Feel the air.
See the texture.

The light

Shoot at late afternoon into blue hour. Use peach highlights, believable skin tones, cool river shadows, and warm practical lights. Retain detail in navy knits and fleece.

The people

Direct natural gestures: laughing on a bench, sharing a drink, leaning on a railing, walking shoulder to shoulder. Mix solo portraits, pairs, and small groups. Keep hands and garment construction believable.

The product

Give one garment a clear lead in each image. Show its silhouette, collar, cuffs, and material. Use cream trousers, denim, and simple footwear to support the layer. Avoid changing its pattern between shots.

ShotPurposeDirection
Wide environmentalHero / billboardBridge and river establish NYC; keep 30–40% calm negative space for copy.
Medium lifestylePrint / feed adsOne or two people; clothing remains easy to recognize at phone size.
Close detailReel / supporting imageFleece pile, ribbed cuff, hand around a cup; shallow depth with sharp material detail.

Include

Weathered wood, bridge railings, navy water, a small evergreen branch, warm string lights, one wrapped gift, or a steaming drink. Choose one or two holiday cues per scene.

Avoid

Heavy snowfall, giant ornaments, neon red and green, tropical scenery, glossy studio lighting, overly orange skin, plastic fabric, exaggerated poses, duplicated people, or distorted fingers.

Choose one believable waterfront viewpoint per scene. Keep bridge geometry and the skyline coherent; do not combine unrelated landmarks into an impossible view.

07 / Composition

One photograph.
One feeling.

Finished campaign assets

  • Lead with one dominant photograph. Use a short headline, approved logo lockup, and one call to action.
  • Align text to one edge. Keep it off the garment and face. Use a cream or navy panel if the photograph is busy.
  • Keep essential content at least 6% inside digital edges. For print, respect the vendor's trim and safe area specifications.
  • Use square corners, fine rules, and generous spacing. Limit torn paper to one restrained edge or label when it helps.

Guide & campaign page

  • Center content in a 1280px container. Use 48px desktop side padding, 28px on tablets, and 20px on mobile.
  • Use a 12-column desktop grid and a simple stacked mobile layout. Space sections 64–96px apart; 32–48px on mobile.
  • Show the full moodboard in the style guide. The campaign hero should be a new single scene, with separate wide and portrait crops.
  • Keep buttons solid evergreen with cream text, clear focus states, and at least 44px touch targets.

Unhurried.
Human.

Campaign copy example

One more walk.

Explore the winter edit

Write like an invitation

Use short, conversational lines about shared time, everyday comfort, and the city slowing down. Let the headline suggest a moment and the supporting line identify the clothing.

Other lines: “Stay for the last light.” / “A little warmth goes a long way.” / “The city can wait a minute.”

Avoid aggressive urgency, luxury clichés, excessive exclamation marks, or invented prices, discounts, fabric performance, sustainability, and product claims.

11 / AI direction

Be specific.
Keep the system.

Attach the moodboard and this guide whenever you generate a new asset. Keep the visual rules fixed and change only the planned subject, scene, shot, or format. Use the following as the starting direction for the assignment's future brand-skills.md and prompt builder.

CAMPAIGN: Faherty — Stay a Little Longer.
CONCEPT: A proposed NYC holiday edit of textured fleece, knitwear, and relaxed overshirts. Comfortable layers invite people to linger outdoors together.
MOOD: Warm, tactile, candid, welcoming, quietly festive.
PLACE: A believable East River waterfront viewpoint with coherent bridge and skyline geometry, weathered benches, and river railings.
COLOR: Waterfront Navy #1D2D44, Warm Cream #F3EBDD; restrained River Blue #627A91, Evergreen #214E43, Fleece Camel #B68A61, Holiday Rust #8B3D35. Preserve reference garment colors.
LIGHT: Late afternoon to blue hour. Warm highlights, cool shadows, natural skin tones, visible fabric texture.
PEOPLE: Adults across ages, body types, and skin tones. Relaxed gestures and believable hands. Keep recurring subjects and garments consistent.
SEASON: One or two small holiday cues; warm lights, evergreen, a gift, or a steaming cup.
COMPOSITION: One focal garment. Calm negative space for typography. No copy over faces or patterned fabric.
TYPE: Georgia Regular headlines; Arial body and labels. Reserve a clean area for the approved logo.
AVOID: Heavy snowfall, neon colors, glossy studio light, orange skin, plastic fabric, duplicated figures, distorted hands, warped architecture, invented product claims.
VARIABLES: [subject] [garment reference] [moment] [shot size] [orientation] [copy space].
OUTPUT: One coherent photograph at the requested aspect ratio. Keep text and logo placement separate from the generated photograph when possible.

Example image prompt

Create a realistic 4:5 lifestyle photograph of two adult friends wearing a navy quilted overshirt and a tan patterned fleece, seated on a weathered bench beside the East River. They share a relaxed laugh over warm drinks. Use a coherent Manhattan Bridge view, late-afternoon peach light, cool blue shadows, and a small strand of warm lights in the background. Show true fleece texture, natural skin, and believable hands. Keep the upper-left area calm for copy. Follow all fixed campaign rules above. No rendered text or logos.

Iteration method

Review the output against the checklist below. Identify a specific problem, ask for that correction, and preserve the successful parts. Save the prompt, output, critique, and revision so the final presentation shows your creative direction.

Example: “Keep the people, crop, and garment pattern. Reduce the orange skin cast, restore navy shadow detail, and remove the oversized ornaments.”

12 / Final review

Does it belong
in this campaign?

  1. The winter layer is clear, with consistent construction, texture, and color.
  2. The scene feels like a believable NYC waterfront location.
  3. Warm highlights and cool blues match the moodboard.
  4. The people feel relaxed, with believable faces and hands.
  5. Holiday details support the scene without taking over.
  6. Copy is short, readable, and free of unsupported claims.
  7. The approved identity, colors, and type remain consistent.
  8. Print, phone, and motion versions fit their actual presentation formats.
